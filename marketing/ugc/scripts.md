# BIOD UGC scripts

Four shoots, one phone, no crew. Vertical 9:16, 15–25 seconds each, shot in
real rooms and a real car. Everything here is checked against the catalogue:
AED 49 / 50 towels, AED 176 / 200 towels (88 fils each, delivery free above
AED 100), AED 36 / 200 tissues (18 fils each).

## Rules that apply to all four

Carried over from the static set, and they matter more on video because the
camera makes them literal.

- **Never say "disposable", "single-use" or "throwaway."** Say *fresh*, *a new
  one*, *unused*. Those three words have read as litter since about 2019.
- **Never film a bin, a used towel, or a hand throwing something away.** Film
  the pull *out* of the box. Dispensing reads as abundance; discarding reads
  as waste.
- **Attack the object, never the viewer.** "It's not you, it's the hook."
  Telling someone they are unhygienic produces a defence of the status quo,
  not a purchase.
- **No skin claims.** Nothing about acne, breakouts, bacteria, dermatologists
  or results. Describe what the product *is*, not what it does to a face.
- **The price is the last beat, never the first** — except in script 2, where
  the count is the whole idea.
- Shoot in a recognisable UAE interior. A generic white bathroom could be
  anywhere and reads as stock.

## 1 · Face towel — "how many times"

**Length** 18s · **Hook type** direct question to camera

| t | Shot | Audio / text |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Close on your own hand lifting the face towel hanging in your bathroom. Hold it up to camera. | "How many times has this touched your face since it was washed?" |
| 3–6s | Still holding it. Half-shrug. | "Six? Ten? I genuinely don't know either." |
| 6–10s | Cut to the BIOD box on the counter. Pull one towel out — slow, deliberate, the fabric unfolding. | "So I stopped guessing." |
| 10–15s | Use it on your face, properly, not a mime. Then it leaves frame — **do not show the bin.** | "A fresh one every time." |
| 15–18s | Box in frame, label readable. | "Fifty in a box. Forty-nine dirhams." |

**On-screen text** (burn in, bottom third): `it's not you. it's the damp hook.`

**Why this one** — it is the only script that gives the viewer a problem
before it gives them a price. The question works because every honest answer
is unflattering, and because she will think about it again tonight in her own
bathroom. That is the actual job: not the click, the reminder.

## 2 · Face towel — "fifty"

**Length** 20s · **Hook type** visual count

| t | Shot | Audio / text |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2s | Overhead, empty counter. Hands place the box down, hard, into frame. | "Forty-nine dirhams." |
| 2–12s | Sped-up overhead: pull towels out one at a time and lay them in a grid across the counter. Let it get slightly absurd. | Count aloud every tenth: "ten… twenty… thirty… forty… fifty." |
| 12–16s | Pull back. The whole counter is covered. You, in frame, looking at it. | "That's one a day for seven weeks." |
| 16–20s | Pick one up, hold to camera. | "Ninety-eight fils each. Ultrasoft bamboo." |

**On-screen text**: `50 towels · AED 49` from 2s, held.

**Why this one** — volume is the easiest thing to prove on camera and the
hardest to argue with. It also pre-empts the "forty-nine for towels?" reaction
by making the viewer watch fifty of them appear.

**Variant worth shooting in the same session**: do it with four boxes and 200
towels for the bundle, end on "eighty-eight fils each, delivered free."

## 3 · Tissue tube — "the cup holder"

**Length** 15s · **Hook type** visual frustration, no dialogue for 6s

This is the angle already working in the account — the ad set runs on car
interests and three traced orders have come through it.

| t | Shot | Audio / text |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4s | In the driver's seat. Try to put a normal rectangular tissue box in the cup holder. It does not go. Try the door pocket. It does not go. Wedge it on the passenger seat. It slides off. | No speech. Just the sounds. |
| 4–6s | Look at camera. Say nothing. | — |
| 6–9s | Pick up the BIOD tube. Drop it into the cup holder. It fits. | *clunk* |
| 9–12s | Pull a tissue out one-handed, like you would at a red light. | "That's it. That's the whole thing." |
| 12–15s | Tube sitting in the holder, car moving, Dubai outside the window. | "Eighteen fils a tissue. Four tubes, thirty-six dirhams." |

**On-screen text**: `a tissue box fits nowhere` at 4s · `18 fils a tissue` at 12s.

**Why this one** — it is a demonstration, not a claim. The failure in the first
four seconds is what makes the fit at six seconds satisfying, and the whole
thing survives with the sound off, which is how most of Reels is watched.

## 4 · Tissue tube — "everywhere a box doesn't go"

**Length** 22s · **Hook type** rapid cuts

| t | Shot | Audio / text |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2s | Tissue box on a kitchen counter, taking up space. Push it aside with one finger. | "A tissue box fits exactly one place." |
| 2–4s | Same counter. The tube, standing. | "This fits everywhere else." |
| 4–16s | Hard cuts, ~2s each, tube being placed: **car cup holder · car door pocket · handbag · desk beside a laptop · bedside table · bathroom shelf.** Same framing each time, same motion. Rhythm matters more than the locations. | Nothing, or one word per cut: "car. bag. desk. bed." |
| 16–19s | Pull one tissue from the desk tube. | "Three-ply bamboo. Two hundred in the pack." |
| 19–22s | Four tubes together on the counter. | "Thirty-six dirhams." |

**On-screen text**: `18 fils a tissue` held from 16s.

**Why this one** — it sells the form factor rather than the tissue, and the
cut rhythm is what carries it. It is also the easiest of the four to reshoot
with different locations once you know which cut people stop on.

## Shooting notes

- **Shoot vertical, lock exposure**, and film everything twice: once with you
  speaking, once silent. The silent cut becomes the Stories version.
- **First frame is the whole decision.** Frame 1 should already show either a
  face or the problem — never a logo, never a title card.
- **No on-screen price before the halfway point** on scripts 1, 3 and 4. Script
  2 is the exception and opens on it deliberately.
- Keep the raw files. A 20-second UGC clip cuts into three 8-second Stories
  variants, and Meta treats those as separate creative.
