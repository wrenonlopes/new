# BIOD UGC scripts — Arabic

Three shoots, Gulf dialect, phone only. Vertical 9:16, 15–25 seconds. Same
catalogue as the English set: AED 49 / 50 towels, AED 176 / 200 towels
(88 fils each, about four months), AED 36 for four tubes (AED 9 a tube).

## Read this before you shoot

**The storefront is still English only.** `shopLocales` returns
`ar · published: false` — the Arabic translation exists in the theme
(`locales/ar.json`, 9.3 KB, written 27 Sep) but the locale has never been
published, so every visitor lands on an English page. Every order the store has
ever taken carries `en-AE` or `en`, including the two most recent, and including
the customer whose shipping name is in Arabic script.

That is not an argument against Arabic creative — it is an argument for
publishing the locale **first**. An Arabic ad that hands off to an English
product page spends its credibility in the half second after the click, and it
does it to the exact audience it just earned. Publishing is one toggle in
Shopify admin → Settings → Languages → Arabic → Publish.

Shoot these when you like. Run them when `ar` is live.

## Dialect

**Khaleeji, spoken the way you would say it to a friend. Not MSA.** Fus-ha in a
UGC video reads as a news bulletin, and the entire mechanism of UGC is that the
person on camera is not broadcasting. Khaleeji is also understood by the
Levantine, Egyptian and Sudanese buyers in the order book, and it signals
*local*, which none of the other registers do.

Transliteration below is practical, not academic — it is there so the lines can
be read aloud by someone who speaks Arabic but reads it slowly.

## Rules carried over from the English set

- **Never say "تستخدم مرة واحدة" / "استخدام واحد" / "ترميها".** Say *جديدة*,
  *نظيفة*, *ما استُخدمت*. Disposal language has read as litter since about 2019
  in Arabic exactly as it does in English.
- **Never film a bin, a used towel, or a hand throwing anything away.** Film the
  pull *out* of the box.
- **Attack the object, never the viewer.** Telling someone they are unhygienic
  produces a defence of the status quo, not a purchase.
- **No skin claims.** Nothing about حبوب, بشرة, التهابات, بكتيريا or results.
- **Price is the last beat.** On these three it is always the closing line.
- Shoot in a recognisable UAE interior. A generic white bathroom reads as stock.

## 1 · منشفة الفندق — "the hotel towel"

**Length** 18s · **Hook** a joke at the object's expense · **Shoot this first**

This is the mechanism with the only conversion record in the account. The
English hotel-bathroom video runs at roughly 4% CTR — the best of anything
live — and has produced the two most recent orders. Everything here is the same
idea, spoken instead of implied.

| t | Shot | Audio | Transliteration | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4s | Hotel bathroom. Lift the folded white towel off the rail, hold it up, look at it the way you'd look at a suspicious invoice. | «منشفة الفندق… الله أعلم وين كانت.» | *minshafat al-funduq… Allah aʿlam wēn kānat.* | The hotel towel… God knows where it's been. |
| 4–7s | Put it down. Half-smile. | «ما أبي أعرف.» | *mā abī aʿraf.* | I don't want to know. |
| 7–12s | Open your bag on the counter. Take out the BIOD box. Pull one towel — slow, let the fabric open. | «أنا أجيب منشفتي معاي.» | *ana ajīb minshaftī maʿāy.* | I bring my own. |
| 12–15s | Use it properly on your face. It leaves frame — **no bin.** | «منشفة جديدة كل مرة.» | *minshafa jadīda kill marra.* | A fresh one every time. |
| 15–18s | The box on the counter, label readable, hotel mirror behind it. | «ثمانية وثمانين فلس للمنشفة.» | *thamānya w thamānīn fils lil-minshafa.* | 88 fils a towel. |

**Burn-in** (bottom third, from 4s): `منشفة الفندق لها تاريخ` — *the hotel towel
has a history.* Matches the new static of the same name, so the still and the
video reinforce each other in the feed.

**Why this one** — it is the only script in either language built on something
that has already sold. The towel is the villain, not the viewer, and the joke
gives permission to agree.

## 2 · كم مرة؟ — "how many times"

**Length** 18s · **Hook** direct question to camera

| t | Shot | Audio | Transliteration | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4s | Your own bathroom. Lift the towel hanging on the hook. Hold it up to camera. | «كم مرة مسحت وجهك بهذي من آخر غسلة؟» | *kam marra masaḥt wayhak b-hādhī min ākhir ghasla?* | How many times have you dried your face on this since it was last washed? |
| 4–7s | Still holding it. Shrug. | «خمس؟ عشر؟ والله ما أدري بعد.» | *khams? ʿashar? wallah mā adrī baʿad.* | Five? Ten? I honestly don't know either. |
| 7–11s | Cut to the box on the counter. Pull one out. | «فـ بطّلت أخمّن.» | *fa baṭṭalt akhammin.* | So I stopped guessing. |
| 11–15s | Use it on your face. Out of frame — **no bin.** | «وحدة جديدة كل مرة.» | *waḥda jadīda kill marra.* | A fresh one every time. |
| 15–18s | Box in frame, label readable. | «خمسين بالعلبة. تسعة وأربعين درهم.» | *khamsīn bil-ʿilba. tisʿa w arbaʿīn dirham.* | Fifty in a box. Forty-nine dirhams. |

**Burn-in** (from 7s): `المشكلة مو فيك. المشكلة في العلّاقة الرطبة.` — *It's not
you. It's the damp hook.*

**Why this one** — every honest answer to the opening question is unflattering,
and she will think about it again tonight in her own bathroom. That is the job:
not the click, the reminder.

## 3 · العلبة المربعة — "the square box"

**Length** 15s · **Hook** visual frustration, no speech for 6s

The tissue ad set carries three traced orders. This is that angle in Arabic.

| t | Shot | Audio | Transliteration | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4s | Driver's seat. Try to fit a square tissue box in the cup holder. No. Door pocket. No. Wedge it on the passenger seat. It slides off. | *(silence — just the sounds)* | — | — |
| 4–6s | Look at camera. Say nothing. | — | — | — |
| 6–9s | Pick up the BIOD tube. Drop it in the cup holder. It fits. | *(clunk)* | — | — |
| 9–12s | Pull a tissue one-handed, the way you would at a red light. | «بس. هذا كل الموضوع.» | *bas. hādha kill al-mawḍūʿ.* | That's it. That's the whole thing. |
| 12–15s | Tube in the holder, car moving, Dubai through the window. | «تسع دراهم للعلبة. أربع علب بـ ستة وثلاثين.» | *tisaʿ darāhim lil-ʿilba. arbaʿ ʿilab bi-sitta w thalāthīn.* | Nine dirhams a tube. Four for thirty-six. |

**Burn-in**: `العلبة المربعة ما تدخل بأي مكان` at 4s — *the square box fits
nowhere* · `9 دراهم للعلبة` at 12s.

**Why this one** — it is a demonstration, not a claim, and it survives with the
sound off, which is how most of Reels is watched. The failure in the first four
seconds is what makes the fit at six seconds satisfying.

## Shooting notes for the Arabic set

- **Check the burn-in before you export.** Several phone editors reverse Arabic
  letter order or break the joins — the text looks like disconnected glyphs
  instead of a word. Type one line, export a one-second test, and look at it on
  the phone before you cut the whole video. CapCut handles Arabic correctly;
  some templates inside it do not.
- **Right-align every burn-in.** Centred Arabic over a moving shot reads as a
  subtitle track, not as a caption.
- **Speak the numbers in Arabic, print them in Western digits.** On screen, use
  `88`, `49`, `176`, `9` — that is how a UAE price tag, a receipt and the
  checkout all write them, and it is what the new Arabic static uses.
- **Say the price once, at the end.**
- **Film everything twice**, once speaking and once silent. The silent cut is the
  Stories version.
- **First frame is the whole decision** — a face or the problem, never a logo.
- Keep the raw files. A 20-second clip cuts into three 8-second Stories variants,
  and Meta treats those as separate creative.
- **Do not mention cash on delivery** in any of these, on screen or in the
  caption. Owner's call, 3 October.
- Write the Meta primary text in Arabic too. An Arabic video under English
  caption copy looks like a translation someone forgot to finish.
